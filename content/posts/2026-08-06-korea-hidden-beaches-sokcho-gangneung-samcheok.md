Korea's best hidden beaches aren't in Busan — and if you're planning a summer trip to Korea right now, knowing about Sokcho beach, Gangneung summer travel, and the barely-discovered coast of Samcheok could completely change your itinerary. I've spent several summers road-tripping the East Sea (what Koreans call the stretch of water the rest of the world calls the Sea of Japan), and every time I mention these three towns to first-time visitors, their eyes go wide. This guide covers exactly what to expect, how to get there, where to stay, and what to eat — so you can stop scrolling and start booking.

---

## Why Korea's East Coast Beats Busan for Hidden Beach Vibes

The East Coast is Korea's best-kept coastal secret, offering emptier sands and sharper scenery than the country's famous southern port city.

Let me be clear: Busan is brilliant. Haeundae Beach on a sunny Saturday is an experience in itself — if you enjoy being wedged between approximately one million umbrellas. But the East Sea coastline stretching north from Gangwon Province is a completely different animal. The water is colder and cleaner, the beaches are longer and less crowded, and the backdrop flips between dramatic granite peaks (thanks to Seoraksan and Odaesan national parks) and open Pacific-blue horizons.

According to [VisitKorea](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr), Gangwon Province receives a significant surge in domestic tourism every July and August, but international visitor numbers remain a fraction of what Seoul and Busan see — meaning the ratio of stunning scenery to actual tourists still firmly favors the traveler willing to make the journey. As of July 2026, the KTX Gangneung line (which also links to the wider east coast bus network) has made the whole region faster to reach than ever.

The short version: **if you want a Korean beach experience that feels real, not staged, head north of Busan.**

## Sokcho: Where Seoraksan Meets the Sea

Sokcho is the rare town where you can hike through a UNESCO-recognized national park in the morning and bodysurf at a beach café by afternoon.

I first arrived in Sokcho on a muggy August morning, fully expecting a sleepy fishing port. What I got instead was a place buzzing with energy — raw seafood markets, an Instagram-famous lighthouse walk, and **Sokcho Beach (속초해수욕장)** stretching out like a postcard nobody had sent yet.

**The beaches you need to know in Sokcho:**

- **Sokcho Beach** – the main stretch; calm waves, good facilities, less packed than Busan's Haeundae even in peak summer.
- **Daepohang Beach** – a quieter cove right next to the famous Daepohang Squid Street (대포항 오징어거리), where you can buy fresh grilled squid for around ₩5,000–₩8,000 a portion (as of August 2026).
- **Ayajin Beach** – a tiny gem north of the city center, popular with surfers and people who specifically want to avoid crowds.

Don't skip **Seoraksan National Park**, just 20 minutes by bus from the city. Admission is **₩3,500 for adults** (as of July 2026 — check [Korea National Park Service](https://english.knps.or.kr) for current fees). The Biryong Falls trail is doable in two hours and gives you the full mountain drama without a brutal climb.

**Getting to Sokcho from Seoul:** Express buses from Seoul's Dong Seoul Bus Terminal run frequently (roughly every 30 minutes during peak season) and take about **2 hours 20 minutes**. The fare is approximately ₩15,000–₩18,000 one way as of August 2026. There's no direct train, so the bus is the move.

## Gangneung: Korea's Coolest Beach Town Has a Café Scene to Match

Gangneung is where Korean beach culture, specialty coffee, and pine forest scenery collide in the most satisfying way possible.

If Sokcho is wild and adventurous, Gangneung is effortlessly cool. The city shot to international fame after hosting the 2018 Winter Olympics, but its summer identity — centered on **Gyeongpo Beach (경포해수욕장)** and the legendary **Anmok Coffee Street (안목커피거리)** — is what keeps people coming back.

**Gangneung summer travel highlights:**

1. **Gyeongpo Beach** – One of Korea's longest beaches at roughly 6km, backed by a lagoon and pine forest. It's popular but never feels suffocating the way Haeundae can. Arrive before 9am or after 5pm for the best light and fewest crowds.
2. **Anmok Coffee Street** – A strip of independent cafés right on the seafront where you can get a specialty latte and watch the waves. This is not a gimmick — the coffee is genuinely excellent and the vibe is unmatched. Expect to pay ₩6,000–₩9,000 for a specialty drink (as of August 2026).
3. **Jeongdongjin** – A 30-minute train ride south of Gangneung, this is famous for the world's only train station on a beach (quite literally — the platform sits meters from the water). The sunrise here is a must.
4. **Ojukheon House** – A 500-year-old Joseon-era house where the face on Korea's 5,000-won note, scholar Yulgok Yi I, was born. It's free to enter and genuinely beautiful.

**Getting to Gangneung from Seoul:** The **KTX** from Seoul Station takes **about 2 hours** and is supremely comfortable. Tickets start around ₩27,000 one way (as of August 2026). Book ahead on [Korail](https://www.letskorail.com) — summer seats sell out fast.

## Samcheok: Korea's Most Underrated Coastline (Seriously, Tell Nobody)

Samcheok is the East Coast town that even many Koreans haven't fully discovered yet, with sea caves, dramatic cliffs, and beaches that feel like a personal secret.

Here's the thing about Samcheok: when I first told Korean friends I was heading there, more than half of them asked why. That's the best possible sign. **Samcheok (삼척)** sits just south of Gangneung and packs more geological drama per square kilometer than almost anywhere else in Korea.

**What makes Samcheok unmissable:**

- **Haesin Park & Isabu Plaza** – A sprawling coastal park with ocean-facing promenades and a dramatic maritime history museum. Entry is free; the views are priceless.
- **Hwanseon Cave (환선굴)** – One of Korea's largest limestone caves, located just 30 minutes inland. The cave stays at around **9°C year-round**, making it a genuinely refreshing retreat on a hot summer day. Admission is **₩5,000 for adults** (as of July 2026 — confirm current rates at [Korea Tourism Organization](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr)).
- **Deungmyeong-hang Beach (등명항)** – A tiny harbor beach with turquoise-tinted water (rare on the East Coast) and a local seafood market where the catch comes in around 6am.
- **Janghohang (장호항)** – Often called "Korea's Napoli" for its blue water and white boats. Transparent kayak tours operate out of here in summer for around ₩20,000 per person.

Samcheok is compact enough to cover in a long day trip from Gangneung, but spending a night here lets you catch the harbor sunrise — genuinely one of the most beautiful moments I've had traveling in Korea.

## How to Plan a 4-Day East Coast Korea Beach Trip

A practical four-day itinerary lets you hit all three towns without feeling rushed or exhausted.

Here's the route that actually works, tried and tested:

1. **Day 1 — Arrive Gangneung:** Take the morning KTX from Seoul. Drop your bags, walk Gyeongpo Beach, hit Anmok Coffee Street for sunset. Stay in Gangneung.
2. **Day 2 — Gangneung → Samcheok:** Catch an early bus to Samcheok (40 min). Do Hwanseon Cave in the morning while it's cool, then kayak Janghohang in the afternoon. Return to Gangneung or stay overnight in Samcheok.
3. **Day 3 — Head North to Sokcho:** Bus from Gangneung to Sokcho (about 1 hour 10 min). Afternoon at Sokcho Beach or Ayajin. Evening: Daepohang night market for grilled squid and raw sea cucumber.
4. **Day 4 — Seoraksan + Bus Back to Seoul:** Morning hike at Seoraksan (Biryong Falls trail). Afternoon bus back to Seoul, arriving in time for dinner.

**Practical tips:**
- Book KTX and express buses in advance, especially for July–August weekends. Korean public holiday weekends (including Liberation Day on August 15) sell out completely.
- Rent a car in Gangneung or Sokcho if you want flexibility — roads are excellent and parking is cheap outside the main beach areas.
- Mobile data: grab a SIM or portable Wi-Fi at Incheon Airport. Navigation is essential for smaller beaches and caves.
- Accommodation: guesthouses and pension-style stays (펜션) are the local norm and typically **₩60,000–₩150,000 per night** depending on season and view (as of August 2026).
