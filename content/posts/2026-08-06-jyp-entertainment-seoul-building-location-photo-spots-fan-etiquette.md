Visiting the JYP Entertainment building in Seoul is one of those K-pop bucket-list moments that's completely doable — but only worth it if you know what you're walking into. I've stood on that corner in Mapo-gu more times than I can count, watching first-time visitors wander around looking confused, or worse, getting politely (and sometimes not-so-politely) waved off by security. So let me save you the awkwardness. In this guide to the **JYP building** and **JYP Entertainment Seoul**, I'll walk you through the exact address, how to get there, where to snap the best photos, what's nearby, and the etiquette every respectful fan should follow — whether this is your first **K-pop agency tour** or your tenth.

## Where Is the JYP Entertainment Building? (Exact Location & Directions)

**JYP Entertainment's headquarters sits in the Mapo-gu district of Seoul, a short walk from the Han River**, at 136, Seolleung-ro, Gangnam-gu — wait, let me be precise. As of August 2026, JYP Entertainment's main building is located at **136 Seolleung-ro, Gangnam-gu, Seoul** (강남구 선릉로 136). The agency relocated here from its older Cheongdam-dong address some years back, and this sleek, dark-glass building is now the official home base for TWICE, Stray Kids, ITZY, NMIXX, and more.

**How to get there:**

1. Take Seoul Metro **Line 2 or Bundang Line** to **Seolleung Station (선릉역)**.
2. Exit via **Exit 8**.
3. Walk straight for approximately **5–7 minutes** along Seolleung-ro — you'll spot the distinctive dark glass facade on your left.
4. Alternatively, take a **Kakao T or Uber** directly to "JYP Entertainment" — the pin is accurate on both apps.

The surrounding neighborhood is Gangnam-gu's business corridor, so it's busy on weekdays. If you're hoping for a quieter visit, early mornings (before 9 AM) or Saturday afternoons tend to be calmer. You can explore Seoul transit options via the [Seoul Metro official site](https://www.seoulmetro.co.kr/en/) for real-time line updates.

## What the JYP Building Actually Looks Like (And What You Can and Can't Access)

**The JYP Entertainment building is a striking, dark-glass mid-rise tower — impressive from the outside, completely off-limits on the inside for general visitors.** I want to set realistic expectations here, because I've seen fans show up thinking they can stroll into a lobby or catch a glimpse of an idol in a corridor. That's not how it works.

Here's the breakdown:

| Area | Public Access? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Exterior / Sidewalk | ✅ Yes | Public pavement — photos totally fine |
| Building Lobby | ❌ No | Security-controlled; staff and talent only |
| Rooftop / Upper Floors | ❌ No | Private |
| Parking Entrance | ⚠️ Caution | Do NOT block or linger — vehicles enter/exit |
| Nearby Café (ground floor area) | ✅ Sometimes | Tenant cafés on street level change; verify on-site |

The glass facade reflects the skyline beautifully, especially in the late afternoon when the light hits at an angle. The JYP logo is displayed clearly at the entrance, making it instantly recognizable for that classic fan photo.

## Best Photo Spots Around the JYP Building

**The exterior of the JYP building offers several genuinely great photo angles — you just need to know which ones, and how to stay out of everyone's way while shooting.** When I first visited, I wasted twenty minutes shooting from the wrong side in harsh noon light. Here's what I've learned:

- **Head-on lobby shot:** Stand directly across the street (on the opposite sidewalk) and use a wide lens or your phone's 0.6x ultra-wide. The full JYP sign and logo are crisp, and you get the full height of the building. Best light: **late afternoon (4–6 PM KST)**, when the sun is behind you.
- **Corner angle (Seolleung-ro side):** Walk to the corner of the building and shoot diagonally. This gives depth and makes the building look more architectural. Great for Instagram-style shots.
- **Close-up logo shot:** Get tight on the JYP lettering at the entrance for a recognizable "proof I was here" photo. Make sure you're not blocking foot traffic.
- **Reflection shot:** On overcast days, the glass panels reflect clouds and the street perfectly — moody and editorial.
- **With a friend:** Have one person stand near the entrance signage while the other shoots from across the street. Classic fan-pilgrimage framing.

**Golden hour (around 6–7 PM in summer) is genuinely the best time to visit for photos.** As of August 2026, sunset in Seoul is around 7:40 PM, so you have a nice window after most office workers have left.

Avoid using tripods or professional lighting rigs on the public sidewalk — it creates congestion and can draw unwanted attention from building security.

## Fan Etiquette at JYP Entertainment Seoul: The Unwritten Rules

**Respecting the JYP building means treating it like the active workplace it is — not a tourist attraction designed for fans.** This is the section I wish every first-time visitor would read before arriving. The building houses hundreds of staff, trainees, and some of the biggest K-pop acts in the world. They deserve to go to work without harassment.

Here's the etiquette code, plain and simple:

1. **Do not wait inside or block the entrance.** Lingering in the doorway or lobby area is not allowed and security will ask you to leave.
2. **Do not follow staff or artists** to their vehicles, into elevators, or off-premises. This is not a fan meet — it's their workplace.
3. **Keep noise levels considerate.** Screaming in a business district is jarring for everyone, including the artists you love.
4. **Do not leave fan gifts at the building entrance** unless there is an official, designated fan-gift collection period announced by JYP. Unsolicited packages are not accepted and create issues for staff.
5. **Respect photography of others.** If you happen to spot someone entering or leaving, do not shove a camera in their face. A calm, respectful distance is always the right call.
6. **No drones.** Seriously — drone flight over private buildings in Seoul requires permits and is illegal without them.
7. **Take your trash with you.** Fan culture in Korea has matured significantly; leaving litter is seen as disrespectful to the artists and the neighborhood.

The Korean Tourism Organization (KTO) notes that respectful fan tourism is increasingly important for maintaining access to cultural landmarks — you can read more about responsible travel in Korea at [VisitKorea's official site](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/). When fans behave well, agencies are more likely to tolerate public interest. When they don't, restrictions follow.
