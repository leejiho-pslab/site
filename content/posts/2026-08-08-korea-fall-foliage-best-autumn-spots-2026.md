Korea fall foliage is, without exaggeration, one of the most photogenic natural events on the planet — and autumn in Korea is the season that finally convinced me to stop hopping between cities and just slow down. Whether you're planning your first trip or your fifth, this guide covers the best spots to see fall colour, the ideal timing for each, how to get there without the headache, and a few hidden gems the tour buses mostly skip.

---

## Why Autumn in Korea Hits Different (and When to Go)

The magic of Korean autumn comes down to dramatic temperature swings that force the leaves to turn fast and vivid. Korea's mountains run north to south down the peninsula, and foliage follows them like a slow-moving wave — starting in the north and high elevations in early October, then washing down to the southern lowlands by early November. **Peak colour at most major sites lands between mid-October and early November**, though year-to-year variation of a week or two is normal depending on summer rainfall and early-autumn temperatures.

Here's a rough timing guide (as of August 2026 forecasts from the Korea Meteorological Administration):

| Region / Spot | Typical Peak Foliage | Elevation |
|---|---|---|
| Seoraksan (Gangwon) | Early–mid October | 1,708 m |
| Naejangsan (Jeollabuk-do) | Late October–early November | 763 m |
| Odaesan (Gangwon) | Mid October | 1,565 m |
| Namhansanseong (Gyeonggi) | Late October | ~480 m |
| Seoul (Bukhansan, Gyeongbokgung) | Late October–early November | Varies |
| Jirisan (South Gyeongsang) | Late October–early November | 1,915 m |

Always cross-check the [Korea Meteorological Administration](https://www.kma.go.kr) closer to your travel dates — their foliage forecast page (단풍 예보) goes live each September.

## Naejangsan: Korea's Undisputed Autumn Capital

**Naejangsan National Park is the single best place in Korea to see fall foliage** — locals call it the "Hollywood of Korean autumn," and after walking its main canyon trail in late October, I completely understand why. The park sits in Jeollabuk-do (North Jeolla Province), about two and a half hours south of Seoul by KTX and bus, and it packs an almost absurd density of maple, oak, and zelkova trees into a tight horseshoe valley.

The classic route is the **Naejangsan Canyon Trail (내장산 단풍터널)** — a 1.4 km canopy walk lined almost entirely with maples. On a bright morning when the reds and oranges are at peak, the light filtering through the canopy is genuinely otherworldly. I'd recommend arriving before 9 a.m. on weekdays; weekend crowds in peak season are real, and the shuttle buses from the main entrance fill up fast.

**Practical details (as of August 2026):**
- Entry fee: ₩3,000 adults (check [Korea National Park Service](https://www.knps.or.kr) for current rates — prices do change)
- Nearest station: Jeongeup (정읍) on the KTX Honam line — then a local bus or taxi (~20 min)
- Accommodation: Jeongeup city has good mid-range options; booking 4–6 weeks ahead in October is strongly advised

## Seoraksan: Dramatic Peaks and the Earliest Colour

Seoraksan turns first, and it turns hard. The granite peaks of this Gangwon-do national park make the backdrop almost cinematic — russet and gold leaves against grey rock faces with a hint of early-winter mist rolling in from the East Sea. **If you want to catch foliage and dramatic mountain scenery together, Seoraksan is your spot**, and the peak usually hits in the first two weeks of October.

The cable car up to Gwongeumseong Fortress is the most popular route for a reason — **views from the top on a clear day stretch to the East Sea** — but the Biryong Falls trail lower down gives you colour at a more leisurely pace and is far less crowded. I'd do both if you have a full day.

**Getting there:** Take the KTX or ITX to Sokcho (most visitors transit through Dong Seoul or Cheongnyangni station). The journey takes around 2.5–3 hours depending on your origin. Sokcho's intercity bus terminal is well-connected. For full transport options, [VisitKorea's Seoraksan page](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) is the most up-to-date official resource.

## Seoul's Hidden Autumn Gems (No Long-Distance Train Required)

Seoul itself does autumn beautifully, and it's easy to underestimate. **You don't need to leave the capital to see world-class fall colour** — you just need to know where to look.

My personal favourites within Seoul and the immediate Gyeonggi area:

1. **Bukhansan National Park** — Take Line 3 to Gupabal and hike up toward Baegundae Peak. The lower forest sections are stunning in late October, and you'll be back in Hongdae for dinner.
2. **Gyeongbokgung Palace grounds** — The ginkgo trees along the main axis turn a brilliant yellow in late October. Combine it with Gyeonghuigung and Deoksugung for a full palace-hopping autumn day.
3. **Namhansanseong (남한산성)** — A UNESCO World Heritage mountain fortress 30 minutes southeast of central Seoul by subway and local bus. The fortress walls winding through maple forest in late October are arguably Seoul's best-kept autumn secret.
4. **Changdeokgung's Secret Garden (Huwon)** — Guided tours only, with limited tickets per day; the lotus pond surrounded by turning trees is one of the most serene spots I've found in any city. Book tickets well in advance via the [Cultural Heritage Administration](https://www.cha.go.kr).

## Getting Around Korea for Foliage Season: Transport Tips

Autumn is one of Korea's three peak domestic travel seasons (alongside Lunar New Year and Chuseok), which means **trains sell out weeks in advance**. Here's how to handle it:

1. **Book KTX tickets as soon as they open** — seats typically go on sale 30 days before departure on [Korail's website](https://www.letskorail.com) or the Korail Talk app.
2. **Consider the Mugunghwa/ITX trains** for routes where KTX doesn't run — they're slower but more scenic and far easier to book last-minute.
3. **Rent a car for Naejangsan and Jirisan** — these parks are more rewarding when you can stop at smaller trailheads that buses skip entirely. International driving permits are accepted in Korea.
4. **Buy a T-money card** for seamless subway and bus travel in Seoul and most major cities — available at any convenience store.
5. **Download Naver Maps** (or KakaoMap) — English navigation is solid on Naver Maps and it integrates real-time public transit updates, which Google Maps still sometimes misses in rural Korea.

## Practical Checklist Before You Go

- ✅ **Check foliage forecast** at [Korea Meteorological Administration](https://www.kma.go.kr) from September onwards
- ✅ **Book KTX tickets** 30 days out — especially for Naejangsan (Jeongeup) and Seoraksan routes
- ✅ **Reserve accommodation** in Jeongeup/Naejangsan area 4–6 weeks ahead in October
- ✅ **Book Changdeokgung Secret Garden tickets** in advance via the Cultural Heritage Administration
- ✅ **Bring layers** — mornings in the mountains drop to near-freezing in late October
- ✅ **Go on weekdays** whenever possible; weekend crowds at top parks are significant
- ✅ **Arrive early** (before 9 a.m.) at Naejangsan and Seoraksan — shuttle buses and parking fill up fast
- ✅ **Check official park websites** for any temporary trail closures or fee updates — policies change seasonally

## The One-Line Takeaway

**If you only have one autumn trip in Korea, go to Naejangsan in the last week of October** — but wherever you end up, the colours are worth every packed train and early alarm. Autumn in Korea is the kind of thing you plan once and then spend the next year figuring out how to come back.
