Busan's beaches — especially **Haeundae** and **Gwangalli** — are the reason millions of travelers make the trip to Korea's second city every summer, and after living here through multiple beach seasons I can tell you they are genuinely different experiences worth understanding before you show up with a towel and no plan. In this guide I'll compare both beaches honestly, walk you through the best food near each one, and help you build a practical day (or two) around the best of Busan's coastline.

---

## Haeundae Beach: Korea's Iconic Shoreline at Full Volume

Haeundae is the undisputed headliner of Korean beach culture — arrive in August and you'll understand why it draws over a million visitors in a single peak weekend.

When people outside Korea picture a Korean beach, they're almost certainly picturing **Haeundae**. The beach stretches roughly **1.5 km** along Haeundae-gu and is backed by a wall of luxury hotels (Signiel, Paradise, Novotel), department stores, and a lively promenade. As of August 2026, the Busan Metropolitan City operates lifeguard stations, freshwater shower facilities, beach volleyball courts, and a designated swimming zone throughout the official summer season (late June–late August). You can find full seasonal info on the [VisitKorea official page for Haeundae](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/whereToGo/locIntrdn/WNEW_130100.do).

What I love about Haeundae is that it never feels like it's trying too hard — it *is* the thing. On a summer morning before 9 a.m. the sand is golden and calm; by noon it's a technicolor carpet of beach parasols. If you're here for the **Busan Sea Festival** (typically held each summer — check the [Busan Tourism Organization](https://www.bto.or.kr/kor/main.do) for 2026 dates), the main stage is right on Haeundae's shore.

**Practical note:** Haeundae station (Metro Line 2, Exit 3 or 5) drops you a five-minute walk from the water. Parking is a nightmare in peak season — take the metro.

---

## Gwangalli Beach: The One Locals Actually Prefer on a Friday Night

Gwangalli trades Haeundae's raw scale for something more cinematic — the Gwangan Bridge glittering over the water is one of the great urban views in all of Asia.

I'll be honest: when a friend who lives in Busan took me to **Gwangalli** for the first time, I felt a little embarrassed I'd spent so many visits defaulting to Haeundae. Gwangalli's beach is shorter (about **900 m**) and the sand is a touch coarser, but the setting is extraordinary. The **Gwangan Bridge** — Korea's longest suspension bridge at **7.4 km** — frames the horizon in a way that makes every sunset photo look like a movie poster.

The strip of restaurants, bars, and pojangmacha (street food tents) running parallel to the beach is one of the most walkable dining corridors in Busan. Unlike Haeundae, the crowd here skews younger and more local — you'll find art students, couples on dates, and groups sprawled on beach mats with convenience-store snacks at midnight. The vibe is **relaxed, creative, and genuinely fun without trying**.

Gwangalli is served by Gwangan station (Metro Line 2, Exit 3 — then a 10-minute walk) or Geumnyeonsan station (Exit 3). The walk along the waterfront from either station is half the experience.

## Haeundae vs Gwangalli: Side-by-Side Comparison

Both beaches are great — here's how to decide which fits your trip best.

| Feature | Haeundae | Gwangalli |
|---|---|---|
| Beach length | ~1.5 km | ~900 m |
| Vibe | Festive, high-energy, resort-style | Laid-back, artsy, local favourite |
| Best time of day | Morning swim + afternoon | Sunset + evening |
| Iconic view | Hotel skyline, open sea | Gwangan Bridge lit up at night |
| Facilities | Extensive (showers, lockers, lifeguards) | Good (showers, lifeguards, less crowded) |
| Metro access | Haeundae Stn (Line 2) | Gwangan / Geumnyeonsan Stn (Line 2) |
| Distance between them | ~20 min by metro or taxi | — |
| Best for | Families, first-timers, festival-goers | Couples, foodies, night owls |

**My verdict:** If this is your first trip to Busan and you have one full day, spend the morning at Haeundae and the evening at Gwangalli. The beaches are close enough that it's a completely doable double.

## What to Eat Near Haeundae: Seafood, Street Food & Morning Markets

The food scene around Haeundae is built for hungry beachgoers — from raw fish markets to fluffy milkfish bread, you won't go hungry.

**Haeundae Traditional Market** (해운대전통시장), about a 10-minute walk inland from the beach, is my first stop every time. It's famous for:

1. **Ssiat hotteok** — a chewy pancake stuffed with seeds, nuts, and brown sugar syrup; the original stall usually has a line but moves fast.
2. **Ganjang gejang** — raw blue crab marinated in soy sauce, sometimes called "rice thief" because it's so addictively good you'll eat three bowls of rice with it.
3. **Mulhoe** — a cold spicy raw-fish soup, distinctly Busan, best eaten seaside.

For a sit-down meal, the seafood restaurants along **Mipo** (the eastern end of Haeundae beach) serve whole grilled fish and haemul pajeon (seafood scallion pancake). Expect to pay roughly **₩15,000–₩30,000 per person** for a solid seafood spread, as of August 2026 — though prices shift seasonally, so check menus on arrival.

## What to Eat Near Gwangalli: Pojangmacha, Milmyeon & Late-Night Eats

Gwangalli's dining strip is where Busan's obsession with noodles, grilled meat, and cold beer meets one of the city's best views.

Walk the **Gwangalli beach road** after dark and you'll stumble across:

- **Milmyeon** (밀면) — Busan's signature wheat noodle dish, served cold in a tangy broth. It's lighter than naengmyeon and incredibly refreshing after a hot day. A bowl runs about **₩9,000–₩12,000** (as of August 2026).
- **Dwaejigukbap** — a pork and rice soup that Busan locals eat at any hour; several 24-hour spots operate near the Gwangalli intersection.
- **Pojangmacha beer tents** — nothing beats sitting in a plastic-roofed tent with a 500ml draft beer (₩4,000–₩5,000), an order of fried chicken, and the Gwangan Bridge lights reflecting on the water. This is peak Busan living.

For coffee and dessert, the cafés lining the back streets behind the beach (especially the Minhakdong area) are strong — many have rooftop terraces with bridge views.

## Practical Day Plan: Doing Both Beaches in One Day

A well-timed day means you catch Haeundae at its best *and* Gwangalli at its most magical — here's how I'd structure it.

1. **7:30 a.m.** — Arrive at Haeundae early for a calm morning swim and a walk along the empty promenade.
2. **9:00 a.m.** — Breakfast at Haeundae Traditional Market (ssiat hotteok + hot coffee from a stall).
3. **10:30 a.m.–1:00 p.m.** — Beach time: swim, sunbathe, explore the BEXCO area or Dongbaekseom island (a small wooded islet at Haeundae's western tip — great for a shaded walk).
4. **1:30 p.m.** — Lunch at a Mipo seafood restaurant (mulhoe or haemul pajeon).
5. **3:30 p.m.** — Metro to Gwangalli (20–25 min). Walk the beach, find your spot on the sand.
6. **5:30 p.m.** — Golden hour on the beach with a cold milmyeon at a nearby noodle shop.
7. **7:00 p.m.** — Gwangan Bridge lights up at sunset; grab a pojangmacha spot for beer and fried snacks.
8. **9:00 p.m.** — Late stroll along the beach strip, café dessert, metro home.

**Pro tip:** The metro runs until around midnight in Busan — so the late-night bridge view is totally doable without a taxi.

## Before You Go: Quick Checklist

- ✅ **Book accommodation early** if visiting July–August — Haeundae hotels sell out months in advance.
- ✅ **Check official beach opening hours and safety flags** at the [Busan Metropolitan City tourism portal](https://tour.busan.go.kr/index.do) before swimming.
- ✅ **Bring reef-safe sunscreen** — Korean summers are intense (UV index regularly hits 9–11 in August).
- ✅ **Download the Kakao Metro app** for real-time Busan subway navigation.
- ✅ **Carry some cash** — pojangmacha and market stalls often don't accept cards.
- ✅ **Go to Gwangalli on a weeknight** if you want a quieter version of the magic.
- ✅ **Prices and festival schedules change** — always verify current details on [VisitKorea](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr) before your trip.

Busan's beaches aren't just a backdrop — they're the whole show. Whether you come for a morning swim at Haeundae or a late-night beer with the Gwangan Bridge glowing behind you, you'll understand pretty quickly why Koreans consider this city the country's most liveable. Pack light, eat everything, and take the metro.
