Several of the show's most iconic backdrops are real, accessible places — here's what I found when I went looking myself.

The **Incheon waterfront** scenes are harder to pin down to a single spot since production used multiple angles, but the general Incheon Port area (near Incheon Station on Line 1) gives you that grey, industrial-port atmosphere the show captured so well.

## How to Plan a Squid Game Filming Location Tour in Seoul (Step-by-Step)

A one-day Seoul drama location tour is genuinely doable if you plan the route right — here's the exact order I'd do it in.

1. **Start at Ssangmun-dong (Dobong-gu)** — Take Line 1 to Ssangmun Station. The residential complex is a short walk. Spend 45–60 minutes exploring the corridors and stairwells.
2. **Head to Noryangjin Fish Market** — Line 1 to Noryangjin Station. Grab breakfast at the market (the raw fish sets are incredible at 8 a.m.) and scout the underground passage areas.
3. **Walk or taxi to Mapo Bridge / Hangang Park** — Best in late afternoon light. The Hangang River parks are free, open, and gorgeous. Pack a convenience store snack and enjoy the view the Squid Game characters never got to.
4. **End the day in Hongdae or Mapo** — These neighborhoods overflow with Squid Game-themed cafes, pop-up merch shops, and K-drama fan spaces. As of August 2026, several pop-ups are running limited-edition experiences — search "Squid Game pop-up Seoul 2026" for the latest.
5. **Optional Day 2: Day trip to Incheon** — Take AREX or Line 1 from Seoul Station (~50 minutes). Walk the Incheon Port waterfront and visit Chinatown for a full day out.

**Estimated transport cost within Seoul:** A T-money card (rechargeable transit card) covers all subway and bus rides. Single metro rides cost **₩1,400–₩1,800** (as of August 2026). The day trip to Incheon adds roughly ₩2,000–₩4,500 each way depending on your route.
